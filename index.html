What counts as a dental emergency

Not every dental problem needs an emergency visit, but some should never wait. Call us right away if you notice any of the following.

  • Severe toothache that does not ease with over-the-counter medicine
  • A cracked, broken, or chipped tooth with sharp pain or bleeding
  • A tooth knocked out or pushed out of position after an injury
  • Swelling in the gums, cheek, or face, or a bump on the gum
  • A lost crown or filling that leaves the tooth exposed
  • Bleeding that continues after an extraction
  • Pain when biting or lingering sensitivity to hot and cold

Who we are

Emergency Dental Service is a Glen Cove practice dedicated to one clear purpose: helping people who are in dental pain or facing a sudden tooth problem. How we approach emergency dentistry

Every emergency visit follows a clear path. We begin by asking about your symptoms, your medical history, and any medications you take. Then we examine the area and, when needed, take digital images so that we can see the tooth, the root, and the surrounding bone. We do not guess. A precise diagnosis helps us choose the right treatment the first time.

Next, we focus on relief. Depending on the cause, that could mean a filling, a temporary repair, drainage of an infection, the start of an emergency root canal, or an emergency tooth extraction. We describe the choices in plain language, including the benefits, the limits, and the expected cost, and we let you decide at your own pace.

Finally, we plan the follow-up. Many emergencies need a second visit to finish a crown, a bridge, or another permanent restoration. We give you a written summary, home care instructions, and a direct line to call if you have questions after you leave.

Emergency Root Canal

A throbbing toothache that wakes you at night often signals that the nerve inside the tooth is infected or inflamed. An emergency root canal removes that source of pain while saving your natural tooth. During the visit, we numb the area, open the tooth, clean out the damaged tissue, and disinfect the inside of the root canals. Many patients are surprised to find that the treatment feels much like getting a filling.

Saving your own tooth is usually better than replacing it, because it keeps your bite stable and your smile natural. After the emergency stage, the tooth often needs a crown for strength, and we will explain the timeline clearly. Signs that you may need an emergency root canal include lasting sensitivity to hot or cold, pain when biting, a darkening tooth, and a swollen gum nearby.
